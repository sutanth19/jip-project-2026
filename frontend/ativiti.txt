Everything is driven by the selected template. If the admin chooses **Tracing**, the form shows tracing-specific fields. If the admin chooses **Reading**, it shows text, timing, and optional audio. This keeps the interface simple and avoids overwhelming the admin.

# Student Experience

For primary school students, the interface should feel like an educational game rather than an exam.

When they answer correctly:

- ⭐ Stars appear.
- 🎉 Confetti animation.
- 🐵 A friendly mascot says "Hebat!"
- 🔊 Positive sound effects.
- 📈 Progress bar fills.

When they make a mistake:

- 😊 Encourage them with messages like "Cuba lagi!"
- 💡 Highlight the correct answer after several attempts.
- 🔁 Offer a retry instead of showing a harsh "Wrong".

This makes the experience more engaging and reduces frustration.

## My biggest recommendation

I would **not** build 500 individual activity pages.

Instead, I would build around **15 reusable activity engines**. Every activity in the system—whether it is for **Huruf Vokal**, **Perkataan KV + KV**, **KVKK**, or **Bacaan dan Pemahaman**—would be created by combining those reusable engines with different content, SK, SP, and Kemahiran.

That approach gives you:

- A consistent experience for students.
- A simple authoring experience for admins.
- Easy maintenance for developers.
- Scalability as the curriculum grows.
- A foundation for AI features like pronunciation assessment, adaptive recommendations, and learning analytics.

I think this architecture fits both the **KPM curriculum structure** and the long-term vision you've described for Digital MoLIB. It is also the approach used by many mature educational platforms: build powerful reusable activity engines, then let educators create unlimited curriculum content on top of them.
